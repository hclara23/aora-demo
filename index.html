Part oneWhat your members do

Open Link 1. Seven steps.

  1. Look at the first screen without tapping anything. Notice the feel of it first.

    Warm linen ground, a serif greeting, bronze section labels, soft corners — a place, not a form. Underneath the feel: next session, credits left, and whether they are cleared for cold water — all above the fold. No streak, no badge, no “you haven’t been in 12 days.” In a mental-health setting a score you can lose punishes the week somebody was unwell.

  2. Tap Book. Pick Contrast Therapy and choose a time.

    The times offered are computed by the same code the server uses — not a list somebody typed. Book one and it disappears for everyone else.

  3. Now book AORA Breath close to that contrast session.

    It refuses, and it names the reason. Breathwork near cold water can cause a blackout with no warning. The database physically cannot store that pair — it is not a warning somebody can click past.

  4. Tap Check in.

    A code with no name and no service on it — it gets read out across a counter. Check-in only opens shortly before the session, so your board means “this person is in the building,” not “this person is awake.”

  5. Put the phone in aeroplane mode, then come back to the app.

    A calm amber line: “You are offline. You can still see your bookings and check in.” Nothing disappears, nothing spins. Tap Check in — it works, and the app says it will send the moment the signal is back. Booking and cancelling politely refuse until then, because a room cannot be promised without asking the server. Turn aeroplane mode off and watch the line go.

  6. Tap Breath, then Start on any session.

    A real paced timer. It re-checks the water rule every second, so it stops itself if the member checks in for a plunge mid-session. Words, not a pulsing animation — it works with the screen off.

  7. Back to Home. Tap the event under Also on, then Save me a seat.

    The seat count drops and holds. Note what is not there: no attendee list. Who else is coming is nobody’s business — attendance at a recovery clinic is not something other clients get to observe.

  8. Home → Shop. Add the tee and the electrolytes, then look at the basket.

    Tax is $2.64, not $4.95 — charged on the taxable item only, because the electrolytes are exempt. Subtotal, tax and total sit above the button that charges them, never on a later screen.

One more, and it is a question for you Home → Your progress. Streaks, points, badges and a leaderboard, working, on invented data. It is there because you asked to see it before ruling on it — read the two streak numbers side by side, because that is the whole argument. More below.
Why the library has no price Tap Library and you will see “included with your membership.” That is deliberate and it protects real money — the reason is in How it earns.

Part twoWhat your front desk does

Switch to Link 2. Eight tabs across the top — swipe them sideways on a phone.

  1. Schedule. Swipe the board sideways.

    Every room against the same hours. The times stay pinned on the left while you scroll. Book from the panel underneath and watch the room fill.

  2. Operations. Read the numbers.

    Ten figures, no charts. Every one carries a sentence saying what to do about it — a number nobody can act on is decoration on a screen a busy person is scanning.

  3. Sales. Read The agent first, then the three figures under it.

    The agent is the software working your list for you. For every person who opted in it says what it would do next — send the welcome, wait for the scheduled email, or hand them to a person because they are qualified, were referred by a member, or have ignored three emails. It never contacts anybody without consent on file, never emails somebody a colleague rang yesterday, and cannot mention the membership until your certificate exists. For the first weeks it only suggests; you switch sending on once you have watched it think. Below it: not “23 leads,” but who you may call, who you may only see, and who asked you to stop.

  4. Memberships. Read the top of the screen.

    It says you cannot sell memberships yet. That is the software working, not a gap. Texas requires a health spa certificate before a membership may even be advertised — so until yours is on file, there is no screen anywhere that can take that money. Tap Preview to see what it becomes afterwards.

  5. Content. Look at the three columns.

    Written → approved → published, as three separate states. Drafts the assistant wrote are labelled as such. Nothing reaches the public until a named person approves it — everything here goes out under your name and your licence.

  6. Community.

    Open events are how somebody who is not a client gets through the door. Guest passes let a member vouch for someone — the cheapest new client there is.

  7. Shop.

    Stock is summed from what was received and sold, never a counter somebody edits. Reorder flags are there, and it says plainly why it will not sell digital products.

  8. Health. The last tab.

    The apps report their own failures here — what kind, on which build, how many phones, and the trail of screens and buttons just before it broke. The same crash from six phones is one row with a six on it. What you will not find is an error message or anything a member typed: the table these land in has no column for either, so a screenshot of this screen cannot leak anything.

Part threeStreaks, points and badges

Working, on invented data, on the Your progress screen. Here is what to look at and what the decision actually is.

The screen shows the same member's attendance counted two ways, side by side. She came 10 times across 7 weeks, with a gap in the middle.

Cannot go down
Weeks you came — 7
Counts distinct weeks attended, ever. A missed week costs nothing, because nothing was taken away.
Resets to zero
Weeks in a row — 3
The conventional streak. The fortnight off erased four weeks of turning up.

That gap is the whole decision. The planning work for AORA recommends against streaks, points, badges and leaderboards for one reason: a score you can lose lands hardest on the person having the worst month — and in a recovery and mental-health setting, that is the person the clinic exists for.

If you want it anyway, this is the shape to want

Every part of the preview is already built the least harmful way, so the decision is not all-or-nothing:

Badges are for things done, never for consistency, and none can be taken away. The four-week badge counts different weeks, not consecutive ones. Points only ever go up. The leaderboard names nobody and nobody else can see a position.

The one to think hardest about The leaderboard. It is the only one that gets worse when other people do well, and it turns attending a recovery clinic into something clients are measured against each other on. The other three can be built so nobody loses anything. A rank cannot.
Nothing has been decided, and nothing is stored The preview computes from attendance every time it is opened. There is no counter saved anywhere, so switching it off leaves no trace and no client who has "lost" a number. It is off in the real product today and reachable only on the sample data.

Part fourWhat is not built yet

So nothing surprises you later.

Waiting on you

Taking money. The payment step stops exactly where the card processor attaches. That needs the AWS account and Square credentials, which need your business documents.

Real sign-in. Members cannot log into a real account yet — that needs the same AWS account. Everything behind the login is built and tested.

Your actual numbers. Every price, the tax rate, the reorder point, the check-in window: placeholders, labelled as such on screen. Where a figure was not known, none was invented.

Deliberate, not missing

Streaks, badges and points are OFF, and reachable only as the preview above. A score you can lose punishes the week somebody was unwell — see Part three and decide.

No social feed, no attendee lists. Who attends a recovery clinic is not public information among your clients.

No paid content tier. See How it earns — it would cost more than it makes.

No out-of-state shipping. One location, so one set of tax rules. The machinery for forty-seven states is built and switched off.

If you take one thing from this: the rules that could close the business — the water rule, the certificate, the escrow, the two-zone separation — are not policies anyone has to remember. They are built so the wrong thing cannot happen.

Part sixThe app as a business of its own

Every table in this system is scoped to a franchise. That was built in from the first migration, not retrofitted.

Which means one deployment can serve more than one facility — and the running cost you will see in a moment does not multiply. A second clinic is a row in a table, not a second bill.

What already supports it
Every location, member, booking, product, lead and event carries a franchise. Seven of the database migrations enforce it. A second facility sees its own data and cannot see yours, because the scoping is in the schema rather than in a filter somebody has to remember to write.
Built
What it costs you to add one
The servers, databases and load balancer are already running. A second facility adds storage and traffic, both small. Your marginal cost is close to nothing until volume grows.
Near zero
What the market pays for this
The platform most facilities use publishes $99–699 per location per month, plus processing and a 20% cut of clients it introduces. That is the number a facility is already used to paying — it is the anchor, not a price we are setting for you.
$99–699 / location / month
What makes it worth more than a generic platform
The compliance is the product. A recovery facility in Texas needs the health spa certificate gate, the escrow rules, the breathwork-and-water separation and the two-database split. No general platform has any of it, and building it is the expensive part — which you would already have done.
The moat
What licensing would make you, honestly A software vendor. That means support obligations when another clinic's front desk cannot book at 7am, a business associate agreement if their clinical data touches it, and liability for their compliance as well as your own. The economics are excellent — near-zero marginal cost against a market rate somebody is already paying. The commitment is real and worth deciding on deliberately.
The safer first version Licence to facilities in Texas first. The spa-act rules built in here are Texas rules; another state's are different, and the system already refuses to guess about states it has not been taught. That is a feature when you are selling it — it will not quietly get somebody else's licensing wrong.
